The Game of Tarocchi

Long before it was used to peer into the future, tarot was Tarocchi—a competitive, high-stakes trick-taking card game played by Renaissance nobility in northern Italy. Explore the physical medium's global pre-history originating from 9th-century Chinese leaf games, its transcontinental journey through Egypt, and the emergence of elite, hand-painted Visconti-Sforza luxury decks. Discover how regional political climates and printing shifts fractured the rules, spawning distinct adaptations from the Tarocco Bolognese to continental French Tarot.

The Esoteric Transformation

In the 18th and 19th centuries, the Enlightenment salon culture of Versailles—cultivated by figures like Madame de Pompadour—collided with a fascination for ancient mystery. Witness the precise moment Antoine Court de Gébelin invented the myth of Tarot's Egyptian origins, sparking a dramatic occult revival. Trace the missing links of card history through Etteilla, the first professional reader to invent card spreads, and Éliphas Lévi, who mapped the cards to the Hebrew alphabet. This transformation culminates in the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn and the iconic, fully illustrated 1909 Rider-Waite-Smith deck.

The Mirror of the Psyche

Today, tarot has transcended traditional fortune-telling to become a highly functional technological and narrative engine. Learn how modern secular readers utilize card layouts as a psychological mirror validating Carl Jung's archetypes of the subconscious. Explore how the deck operates as a computational relational database, acts as a training benchmark for multimodal AI models, and serves as a powerful content randomizer used to dynamically shape plots in tabletop roleplaying games like Dungeons & Dragons.
